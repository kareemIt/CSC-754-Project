CSC Survey Paper — Master Notes

Smart City Modeling & Simulation Survey

1. Assignment

The project is a survey paper. The goal is to choose a topic, search recent academic literature, catalog the publications, closely read the most relevant papers, classify them, and ultimately write a state-of-the-art survey. The professor recommends using ACM Computing Surveys as the stylistic model.

The final survey should typically contain:

  • 4 foundational papers
  • 8 recent and relevant journal papers
  • 8 recent and relevant conference papers / technical reports

2. Our Topic

Modeling and Simulation for Smart Cities: Methods, Applications, and Emerging Technologies

Central research question

How are modeling and simulation techniques used to understand, design, optimize, and manage smart cities?

Rather than making the survey exclusively about digital twins, we're treating digital twins as one important component of the broader smart-city modeling and simulation field.

3. Half-Page Topic Description

Modeling and Simulation for Smart Cities: Methods, Applications, and Emerging Technologies

As cities continue to grow and face more and more complex infrastructural, energy, transportation, and environmental challenges. So, with the use of smart city simulation, we will be able to understand, design, optimize, and help manage these cities better without the need to test these solutions in the real world.

In this survey, we will be looking at major modeling/simulation methods used in smart city research. This may include agent-based modeling, discrete-event, system dynamics, and data-driven simulation. It will also look into transportation and traffic management alongside energy and smart grids, as well as new and emerging technologies such as digital twins and AI-driven solutions.

The goal of this survey is primarily to classify and compare different approaches to the solutions that current cities are facing and weigh the pros and cons of each approach as we try to determine which set of approaches would be most effective at solving these issues.

4. Planned Classification Scheme

This is currently our working classification for organizing the literature.

  1. I. Foundations & Urban Modeling Architectures
    General smart-city modeling frameworks, urban digital twins, integrated modeling platforms, definitions and architectures.
  2. II. Modeling & Simulation Methods
    Agent-based modeling, system dynamics, numerical/physics-based simulation, discrete-event simulation, microsimulation, cellular models, data-driven simulation.
  3. III. Transportation & Urban Mobility
    Traffic flow, autonomous vehicles, multimodal transportation, routing, congestion, evacuation, and mobility digital twins.
  4. IV. Energy & Buildings
    Urban energy consumption, smart grids, building energy simulation, microclimates, regional energy systems, and energy digital twins.
  5. V. Environment & Urban Resilience
    Flooding, stormwater, climate change, waste management, disaster response, and infrastructure resilience.
  6. VI. AI & Data-Driven Simulation
    Machine learning, generative AI, IoT/sensor integration, real-time models, and intelligent digital twins.
  7. VII. Challenges & Future Research
    Interoperability, model accuracy, scalability, data quality, privacy, standardization, real-time integration, validation, and future research.

5. 50 Articles | Literature Review – Core Smart-City Digital Twin and Simulation Papers

A. Surveys, foundations & general urban digital twins

  1. Smart City Based on Digital Twins — Li, Deren; Yu, Wenbo; Shao, Zhenfeng (2021), Computational Urban Science. Excellent overview of how digital twins relate to smart-city systems and applications.
  2. A Systematic Review of a Digital Twin City: A New Pattern of Urban Governance Toward Smart Cities (2021), Journal of Management Science and Engineering. Covers simulation, IoT, AI, blockchain, and digital-twin city frameworks.
  3. A Digital Twin Smart City for Citizen Feedback — White et al. (2021), Cities. Particularly useful because the model includes flood and crowd simulations.
  4. Urban Digital Twin Applications as a Virtual Platform of Smart City — Yang & Kim (2021). Analyzes and classifies 40 urban digital-twin applications.
  5. The Adoption of Urban Digital Twins — Ferré-Bigorra, Casals & Gangolells (2022), Cities. Maps existing UDT implementations and their structures, applications, inputs and outputs.
  6. Challenges of Urban Digital Twins: A Systematic Review and a Delphi Expert Survey — Lei et al. (2023), Automation in Construction. Identifies major technical and implementation challenges.
  7. Digital Twin-Supported Smart City: Status, Challenges and Future Research Directions — Wang et al. (2023), Expert Systems with Applications. Reviews 202 smart-city digital-twin papers.
  8. Urban Digital Twin Challenges: A Systematic Review and Perspectives for Sustainable Smart Cities (2023), Sustainable Cities and Society. Particularly valuable because modeling, simulation and decision support are one of its main challenge categories.
  9. Digital Twin in Smart City (2023), Transportation Research Procedia. Explicitly discusses modeling and simulation as part of smart-city digital twins.
  10. Digital Twins in City Planning — Michael Batty (2024), Nature Computational Science. This is one I strongly want in our main 20–25; it connects aggregate models, agent-based models, cellular models and microsimulation to digital city twins.
  11. Comprehensive Analysis of Digital Twins in Smart Cities: A 4200-Paper Bibliometric Study (2024), Artificial Intelligence Review. Analyzes more than 4,200 publications, which will also be extremely useful for finding additional literature.
  12. A Review of Urban Digital Twins Integration, Challenges, and Future Directions in Smart City Development — Mazzetto (2024), Sustainability.
  13. Navigating Urban Complexity: The Transformative Role of Digital Twins in Smart City Development (2024), Sustainable Cities and Society. Systematic review based on 64 selected studies.
  14. The Digital Landscape of Smart Cities and Digital Twins: A Systematic Literature Review of Digital Terrain and 3D City Models in Enhancing Decision-Making (2024). Includes physical/environmental simulation and 3D city models.
  15. A Comprehensive Review of Digital Twin Technologies in Smart Cities (2025), Digital Engineering. Covers transportation, energy, emergency response, environmental monitoring and other smart-city domains.
  16. Global Perspectives on Digital Twin Smart Cities: Innovations, Challenges, and Pathways to a Sustainable Urban Future (2025), Sustainable Cities and Society. Strong recent state-of-the-art survey.
  17. IoT, AI, and Digital Twins in Smart Cities: A Systematic Review for a Thematic Mapping and Research Agenda (2025), Smart Cities. Reviews 64 studies.
  18. Towards Connected Simulations – Tracing the Evolutionary Path of Urban Simulations across Digital Twin Projects and Use Cases (2025), Procedia Computer Science. This is extremely on-topic because it explicitly deals with integrating urban simulations into urban digital twins.

B. Transportation & urban mobility simulation

  1. Agent-Based Modeling of a Stadium Evacuation in a Smart City — Zhang et al. (2018), Winter Simulation Conference. Uses agent-based simulation to study smart-city evacuation behavior.
  2. A Systematic Review of Agent-Based Models for Autonomous Vehicles in Urban Mobility and Logistics: Possibilities for Integrated Simulation Models (2021). Excellent bridge between agent-based simulation and future smart-city transportation.
  3. Digital Twins for Urban Mobility — Bachechi (2022). Covers Traffic & Air Quality and multimodal mobility digital twins built from simulation models.
  4. Urban Digital Twins: Decision-Making Models for Transportation Network Simulation — Zhang & Zhang (2022), ACM conference paper. Directly compares digital-twin approaches with conventional transportation simulation.
  5. Traffic Flow Digital Twin Generation for Highway Scenario Based on Radar-Camera Paired Fusion — Li & Zhang (2023), Scientific Reports. Builds a data-driven traffic digital twin.
  6. DTUMOS, Digital Twin for Large-Scale Urban Mobility Operating System — Yeon et al. (2023), Scientific Reports. Another one I strongly favor: validated using Seoul, New York City and Chicago, with more than 20,000 simulated vehicles.
  7. Generative AI-Driven Digital Twin Architecture for Urban Mobility Simulation and Decision Support (2026), Smart Cities. Very current work combining GenAI with mobility simulations.

C. Energy, buildings & urban climate simulation

  1. Sustainable and Resource Efficient Cities Platform – SureCity Holistic Simulation and Optimization for Smart Cities (2019), Journal of Cleaner Production. Develops a holistic simulation/optimization platform for urban energy strategy.
  2. Smart City Digital Twin–Enabled Energy Management: Toward Real-Time Urban Building Energy Benchmarking — Francisco, Mohammadi & Taylor (2020), Journal of Management in Engineering.
  3. On the Design of an Urban Data and Modeling Platform and Its Application to Urban District Analyses — Eicker et al. (2020), Energy and Buildings. Important smart-city modeling platform paper.
  4. Information Modelling for Urban Building Energy Simulation — A Taxonomic Review — Malhotra et al. (2022), Building and Environment.
  5. Urban Energy Simulations Using Open CityGML Models: A Comparative Analysis — Malhotra et al. (2022), Energy and Buildings. Simulates 500 3D buildings using CityGML/Modelica.
  6. Urban Building Energy and Microclimate Modeling — From 3D City Generation to Dynamic Simulations (2022), Energy. Integrates microclimate and building energy simulation.
  7. Development and Application of Digital Twin Technology for Integrated Regional Energy Systems in Smart Cities (2022), Sustainable Computing: Informatics and Systems.
  8. The Spatial Data Infrastructure of an Urban Digital Twin in the Building Energy Domain Using OGC Standards (2022), ISPRS. Uses measured and simulated urban-building energy data.
  9. How Close Are Urban Scale Building Simulations to Measured Data? Examining Bias Derived from Building Metadata in Urban Building Energy Modeling (2022), Applied Energy. Includes simulations covering more than 50,000 buildings.
  10. Building Energy Simulation and Its Application for Building Performance Optimization: A Review of Methods, Tools, and Case Studies (2023), Advances in Applied Energy. Explicitly covers urban-scale simulation and digital twins.
  11. Empowering Smart Cities with Digital Twins of Buildings: Applications and Implementation Considerations of Data-Driven Energy Modelling in Building Management (2024). Compares simulation-based and data-driven building models.

D. Environment, water, floods & resilience

  1. Using a Digital Twin to Explore Water Infrastructure Impacts During the COVID-19 Pandemic (2021), Sustainable Cities and Society. Uses digital-twin modeling for urban water infrastructure.
  2. Designing a Spatially-Explicit Urban Digital Twin Framework for Smart Water Infrastructure and Flood Management — Sabri et al. (2023), IEEE conference. Uses geosimulation, GIS, GeoAI and spatial modeling.
  3. Application of Artificial Intelligence in Digital Twin Models for Stormwater Infrastructure Systems in Smart Cities (2024), Advanced Engineering Informatics.
  4. Climate Change Scenario Simulations for Urban Flood Resilience with System Dynamics Approach: A Case Study of Smart City Shanghai (2024), International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction. Very good example of scenario-based simulation.
  5. The Synergistic Interplay of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Twin in Environmentally Planning Sustainable Smart Cities: A Comprehensive Systematic Review (2024), Environmental Science and Ecotechnology.
  6. Searching for New Urban Metabolism Techniques: A Review Towards Future Development for a City-Scale Urban Metabolism Digital Twin (2024), Sustainable Cities and Society.
  7. Urban Digital Twin-Based Solution Using Geospatial Information for Solid Waste Management (2024), Sustainable Cities and Society. Includes waste-generation simulation and optimized collection routing.
  8. City-Scale Digital Twin Framework for Flood Impact Analysis: Integrating Urban Infrastructure and Real-Time Data Analytics (2025), Urban Climate. Simulates flood effects on transportation and infrastructure.
  9. FlowsDT: A Geospatial Digital Twin for Navigating Urban Flood Dynamics (2026), Computers, Environment and Urban Systems. Uses coupled hydrodynamic simulation and an interactive urban digital twin.
  10. A Numerical Modelling-Supported Digital Twin for Urban Floods Monitoring in Typhoon or Storm Scenario (2026), Environmental Modelling & Software. Combines numerical flood simulation with 3D visualization.

E. System dynamics, AI & integrated smart-city modeling

  1. “Cities Go Smart!”: A System Dynamics-Based Approach to Smart City Conceptualization — Nunes et al. (2021), Journal of Cleaner Production. Uses system dynamics to model causal relationships among smart-city determinants.
  2. Research on System Dynamics Model and Computer Simulation of Smart City System — Cheng & Li (2022). Explicit smart-city system dynamics simulation.
  3. System Dynamics Modeling of Collaborative Governance in Smart Cities: A Case Study of Dongguan, China — Liu et al. (2024), Scientific Reports. Creates a dynamic model of interacting smart-city subsystems.
  4. Leveraging Generative AI for Urban Digital Twins: A Scoping Review on the Autonomous Generation of Urban Data, Scenarios, Designs, and 3D City Models for Smart City Advancement — Xu et al. (2024), Urban Informatics. Very useful recent AI paper covering transportation, energy, water and infrastructure modeling.

7. Main Argument Beginning to Emerge

We haven't finalized a thesis yet, but the literature is pointing toward a useful story:

Smart-city simulation is evolving from relatively isolated and static urban models toward integrated, data-driven, continuously updated systems.

The progression we may eventually discuss is approximately:

Traditional simulation → integrated urban modeling → sensor/IoT-connected models → digital twins → AI-enhanced and increasingly real-time simulation.

At the same time, older simulation methods aren't simply disappearing. Agent-based modeling, system dynamics, numerical simulation, microsimulation, etc. increasingly operate inside larger digital-twin/smart-city platforms.

This distinction could become one of the central ideas of our final survey.

8. Questions We'll Compare Across Papers

  • Research problem — What problem is being solved?
  • Application/domain — Transportation, energy, flooding, planning, etc.
  • Modeling technique — Agent-based, system dynamics, numerical, ML/data-driven, etc.
  • Simulation purpose — Prediction, optimization, scenario testing, decision support, monitoring, etc.
  • Data — Historical, synthetic, IoT sensors, GIS, cameras, building data, etc.
  • Scale — Building, neighborhood, transportation network, or entire city.
  • Real-time capability — Static/offline vs continuously updated.
  • Validation — How do the authors establish that their model works?
  • Main results — What did they actually discover?
  • Strengths — Why is the approach useful?
  • Limitations — What doesn't it solve?
  • Future work — What do the authors say needs to happen next?

These will become our literature matrix.
